### Task 08.03 - Course Review

*How would you rate the difficulty of this lecture from 1 (far too easy) to 5 (far too difficult)?*

4 - as a person without many academic writing practice.

<br>

*How would you rate the amount of work you had to put into this lecture so far from 1 (no work at all) to 5 (far too much work)?*

3

<br>

*Which one was your favorite chapter, which one your least favorite?*

Favourite: "03 Research," because I am most interested in how I can practice research as an artist throughout my creative process and, furthermore, how this can take place in an academic context. This includes the idea of framing my work and process as a written investigation, or developing it with a more conscious awareness of intent. Through the conference in Turin, I gained a better understanding of how to further develop discourse and research in general.


Less favourite: statistics / qualitative and quantitative research - because it felt a bit plain.

<br>

*Do you feel well prepared to write your short paper? If not, what was missing?*

I feel that we have all needed information in the lectures to write a short paper, but the practice is still missing. But as always, you never really feel prepared unless you do it :)

<br>

*Please feel free to add any feedback you want to give!*

The structure of the course and the assignments felt very coherent to me and built on each other very well. The assignments also provided a good challenge to engage with the development early on and to refine the process, for which I am now very grateful at the end of the semester.

What I sometimes found challenging was the course’s rather lecture-based approach. The content felt consistently prepared (which provided a good overview of all the material) but also quite dense. Within this basic structure, I would have appreciated more interactive formats as a refreshment, e.g. conducting our own research on papers and discussing topics based on existing publications. But this point is also due to the theoretical and methodological focus of the class.
